# Dialogue

Use when meaning develops through alternating speakers, questions and answers, objections and responses, or distinct points of view. Do not use for unrelated quotations shown in sequence.

## Semantic roles

- `[data-motion-role="speaker"]` — a stable identity, name, or avatar region.
- `[data-motion-role="turn"]` — the current spoken or written contribution.
- `[data-motion-role="response"]` — the answer, reaction, or consequence of the prior turn.
- `[data-motion-role="context"]` — shared topic or evidence that remains available.
- `[data-motion-role="resolve"]` — the agreement, decision, or remaining tension.

## Registry candidates

Prefer `speaker-intro`, `split-screen`, `expert-panel`, `profile-quote`, `comment-thread`, `douyin-comment-stack`, `social-comment-highlight`, `x-space`, or `lt-clean-bar`. Combine an identity component with a conversation component only when both remain editable and share the project timeline.

## Beat grammar

- **Establish:** identify the participants and shared question before the first response.
- **Develop:** alternate turns in source order. Shift emphasis to the active speaker while preserving enough context to understand the reply.
- **Land:** display the decision, contrast, or unresolved question produced by the exchange.

## Execution contract

One beat equals one meaningful turn or reaction. When real narration is available, bind each turn to the measured speaker segment. Without authorized narration, use estimated reading time and visual speaker labels; continue silently without inventing audio. Do not animate a new speaker before the prior turn is understandable.

Use position consistently: participants must not swap sides unless the story explicitly changes location. A response must visually refer to its prompt through alignment, connector, quote reference, or retained context. Long monologues should switch to another pattern or split into subshots.

## Acceptance

- Every turn has an identifiable speaker or source and an observable active state.
- Questions and responses remain in source order and are distinguishable at direct seeks.
- Silent output remains understandable through labels and text timing.
- The Land frame communicates the outcome of the exchange rather than freezing on an arbitrary speaker.
